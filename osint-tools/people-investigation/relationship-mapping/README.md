# Relationship Mapping

## Overview

Relationship mapping tools help identify and visualize connections between a person and their family, professional network, or known associates. This subcategory carries a particular ethical consideration beyond the rest of `people-investigation/`: it necessarily extends research to third parties (family members, colleagues, associates) who may not themselves be the subject of any legitimate investigation. Apply the proportionality standard from the [people-investigation category overview](../README.md) with particular care here, limiting research into third parties to what is genuinely necessary for your documented purpose.

---

## Public Record-Based Relationship Discovery

| Technique/Resource | Description | Best For | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Property co-ownership records | County/local property records often disclose co-owners of a property, which can indicate a household, business, or family relationship | Identifying shared property interests relevant to a due diligence or asset investigation | Free/Paid, varies by jurisdiction (see `search-and-discovery/government-databases/`) |
| Marriage and divorce records | Where publicly available, these records establish a legal spousal relationship and, in divorce filings, sometimes disclose additional relevant relationship or asset detail | Confirming a claimed marital relationship as part of a documented investigation | Free/Paid, varies by jurisdiction |
| Business co-registration records | Corporate and business registries often disclose co-founders, co-officers, and registered agents | Identifying professional/business relationships relevant to a business intelligence investigation | Free, see `search-and-discovery/government-databases/` and `business-intelligence/company-research/` |
| Probate and estate records | Public probate filings can disclose family relationships relevant to inheritance | Genealogical research or asset investigation involving an estate | Free/Paid, varies by jurisdiction |

## Genealogy and Family History Platforms

| Tool | Description | Best For | Cost |
|---|---|---|---| 
| FamilySearch | Free, large-scale genealogical records database maintained by a nonprofit organization | Historical family relationship research using publicly contributed genealogical records | Free |
| Ancestry | Commercial genealogy platform with extensive historical record digitization | In-depth historical family history research | Paid subscription |
| MyHeritage | Commercial genealogy platform with historical records and DNA matching services | Family history research, including international records | Freemium/Paid |

## Professional Network Mapping

| Tool/Technique | Description | Best For | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn connection and "People Also Viewed" review | Reviewing a public professional profile's disclosed connections and related profiles | Understanding a person's professional network as publicly disclosed | Free (enhanced with paid LinkedIn tiers) |
| Company "leadership team" and organizational chart pages | Many companies publish their leadership structure publicly | Establishing formal organizational/reporting relationships | Free |
| Conference co-presentation and publication co-authorship review | Reviewing academic or professional conference programs and publication author lists | Establishing professional collaboration relationships | Free, using tools in `search-and-discovery/academic-resources/` |

## Visualization

Once relationships are identified, visualize them using the graph tools documented in `social-media-intelligence/network-mapping/` (Gephi, Maltego, Cytoscape) or the dedicated link-analysis tools in `data-visualization/link-analysis/`.

## Usage Notes

- A shared address, shared property, or shared business registration is a data point suggesting a relationship, not proof of the nature or currency of that relationship; people move, sell shared property, and end business relationships, and public records can lag these changes substantially.
- Genealogy platforms' data quality varies significantly and often includes user-contributed information that has not been independently verified; corroborate any significant finding against an official public record where possible.

---

## Legal and Ethical Notes

- Limit relationship mapping to what is genuinely necessary for your documented investigative purpose. Mapping a subject's entire family and social network when your actual information need is narrow (for example, confirming a single specific business relationship) is disproportionate and inconsistent with this repository's [Code of Conduct](../../../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
- Third parties identified through relationship mapping (family members, associates) have their own privacy interests. Absent a specific, legitimate reason to investigate a third party directly, limit your work product to noting the relationship itself rather than conducting a full independent investigation into every connected individual.
- Never use relationship mapping to identify a path to reach, locate, or exert pressure on a person who has taken steps to avoid contact with a specific individual, including through their family members or associates.
- Genealogical DNA-matching services carry additional privacy considerations, since they can reveal biological relationships unknown to the parties involved; exercise particular care and, where relevant, awareness of the platform's own consent and law-enforcement-access policies before relying on such a service in an investigation.
