# External Learning Resources

## Purpose Statement

This is a curated, non-exhaustive list of external OSINT learning resources: books, training providers, practitioner communities, conferences, and newsletters. Inclusion here is not an endorsement of every view expressed by a given resource; this list reflects a general assessment of educational value and sustained relevance to the field. As with all external references in this repository, verify current availability and details independently, as resources change over time.

---

## Books

| Title | Author | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| *Open Source Intelligence Techniques* | Michael Bazzell | Comprehensive, frequently updated practical technique reference; a long-standing staple of the field |
| *Hiding from the Internet* | Michael Bazzell | The privacy/protective counterpart to OSINT technique — useful for understanding both sides of the discipline |
| *We Are Bellingcat* | Eliot Higgins | Narrative account of Bellingcat's investigative methodology and notable cases |
| *The OSINT Handbook* | i-intelligence | Practical reference covering a broad range of tools and techniques, oriented toward humanitarian and development contexts |

## How to Evaluate a New Resource Before Relying on It

- **Track record:** Has this source been consistently accurate and current over time, or is it new and unproven?
- **Transparency about methodology:** Does the resource explain its techniques and reasoning, or just assert conclusions?
- **Currency:** OSINT tooling changes quickly; prefer actively maintained resources over static ones for tool-specific guidance.
- **Alignment with ethical practice:** Does the resource emphasize legal and ethical boundaries consistent with `osint-templates/operational-planning/legal-compliance-checklist.md`, or does it treat those boundaries as an afterthought?
